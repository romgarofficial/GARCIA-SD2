Ada Lovelace

Software Engineer

Ada Lovelace was a 19th-century mathematician and writer who is now recognized as the world's first computer programmer. As the daughter of the poet Lord Byron, she was raised by her mother to study mathematics and science in an attempt to suppress any "inherited" artistic moodiness. In her late teens, she began a lifelong collaboration with inventor Charles Babbage, who designed a mechanical general-purpose computer called the Analytical Engine.

While translating an article about the engine in 1843, Lovelace added extensive "Notes" that included a detailed algorithm for calculating Bernoulli numbers, widely considered the first published computer program. Beyond this, she was a visionary who realized that computers could manipulate more than just numbers; she predicted they could one day compose music or create art. She described her approach as "poetical science," blending her father’s imagination with her mother’s mathematical rigor. Though she died at only 36, her legacy was rediscovered in the 20th century, and the U.S. Department of Defense named the Ada programming language in her honor.

Note G

In 1843, Lovelace wrote a series of notes on Charles Babbage’s proposed Analytical Engine. In "Note G," she detailed an algorithm to calculate a sequence of rational numbers known as Bernoulli numbers. Because this was a set of instructions designed specifically to be "read" and executed by a machine, it is recognized as the first computer program in history.
